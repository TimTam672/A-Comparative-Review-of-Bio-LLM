# Biological Large Language Models: A Comparative Review of Protein and DNA Foundation Models for AI-Driven Scientific Discovery

![article](https://github.com/TimTam672/A-Comparative-Review-of-Bio-LLM/blob/5c3d017a307913bf1766bf4e2abe1079c70dd700/A%20Comparative%20Review%20of%20Protein%20and%20DNA%20Foundation%20Models%20for%20AI-Driven%20Scientific%20Discovery.pdf)

## Abstract

Large language models have moved from natural language into molecular biology, where protein and genomic foundation models now drive a growing share of AI-assisted discovery. Existing reviews examine these two model families in isolation, leaving their shared architecture and their differences underexplored. This review compares protein language models (pLMs) and DNA language models (dLMs) within a common AI-for-science framing. It traces the transformer architectures that underpin both families, contrasting encoder-only masked-language models with decoder-only generative models, and examines how the choice of tokenization shapes each family's capacity to capture long-range dependencies. The comparison extends to zero-shot learning, which determines how each family performs when labeled data are scarce. Representative models and their downstream applications are surveyed, including protein structure prediction, inverse folding, variant effect prediction, de novo design, and the prediction of regulatory and epigenomic elements. A comparative analysis of benchmark performance across protein structure, multi-modal protein, and DNA tasks indicates that architecture and tokenization choices, rather than scale alone, account for much of the observed variation. The review concludes that tailored tokenization and zero-shot learning are central to progress, and that these models carry particular value for variant pathogenicity analysis and precision medicine.
